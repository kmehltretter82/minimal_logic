We prove the inductive lemma of p. 114, lines 14–22. Let $s$ be generated by clauses (1)–(3) over distinct variables $F_1,\dots,F_k:\nu$ and $\alpha:0\to0$. Write $s[\bar v]$ for the result of substituting the Church numerals $\bar v_i$ for the $F_i$. Then either $s=\alpha$, or there are a polynomial $P$ and a variable $z$ of type $0$ such that, for all $v$ with every $v_i\neq 0$,

$$s[\bar v]\ \triangleright\ \lambda y.\ \alpha^{P(v)}z,$$

where $z$ is $y$ or a free variable of $s$.

**Idea.** A numeral $\bar n$ iterates its argument $n$ times. A term of type $0\to0$ reduces to one of two kinds of function:

- *bound:* the shift $\lambda y.\alpha^{Q}y$;
- *constant:* $\lambda y.\alpha^{Q}w$ with $w\ne y$.

Iterating a shift $n$ times multiplies the shift by $n$. Iterating a constant function at least once gives the same constant. So we prove a sharper classification, fixed uniformly in $v$: $s$ is $\alpha$, or $s$ is bound with $s[\bar v]\triangleright\lambda z_0.\alpha^{P(v)}z_0$, or $s$ is constant with $s[\bar v]\triangleright\lambda y.\alpha^{P(v)}w$, where $y\ne w$ and $w$ is free in $s$.

**Reductions used.**

$$\bar n\,t\ \triangleright\ \lambda u.\,t^n u\quad(u:0 \text{ not free in } t),$$

$$(\lambda y.\alpha^{Q}y)^n\,u\ \triangleright\ \alpha^{nQ}u,$$

$$(\lambda y.\alpha^{Q}w)^{n+1}\,u\ \triangleright\ \alpha^{Q}w \quad (w\neq y).$$

In each of these, substitution happens only into terms without abstractions, or substitutes closed numerals, so the equations hold literally.

**Induction.** The proof is a simultaneous structural induction on the three grammar predicates.

- *The variable $\alpha$* is the first alternative.
- *$F_i\,s'$.*
  - If $s'=\alpha$, then $F_i\alpha$ is bound with $P=v_i$.
  - If $s'$ is bound with $Q$, then $F_i s'$ is bound with $P=v_i\cdot Q$.
  - If $s'$ is constant, then $F_i s'$ is the same constant. This uses $v_i\ge 1$.
- *Chains $s_1(\dots(s_qz)\dots)$.* The innermost variable gives $\alpha^0 z$. Then each $s_j$ acts on $\alpha^{P'}w'$:
  - $\alpha$ adds $1$;
  - a bound $s_j$ with $Q$ gives $\alpha^{Q+P'}w'$;
  - a constant $s_j$ replaces the value with its constant $\alpha^Qw$.
- *$\lambda y.\,\mathrm{chain}$.* The result is bound if the chain's final variable is $y$, and constant otherwise.

**Conclusion.** In the bound case take $z=y=z_0$. In the constant case take $z=w$, which is free in $s$.

The proof is constructive and uses only `propext` and `Quot.sound`.
